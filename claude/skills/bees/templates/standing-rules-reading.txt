Read yourself only what was named to you — the brief, its plan section, a report, or a file:line a
scout returned — at most 60 lines a read. Send every other lookup (a grep, a find, a file or range
nobody named) to a scout, `bee-scout` or `scout` under codex, and spawn no other sub-agent, because
a scout is faster and cheaper than looking yourself: its model is fast and cheap, any number run in
parallel, and its report lands once while your own output stays in context every turn. Your first
wave maps the whole change, one scout per question, all at once: declarations, copy and clone
helpers, callers of every signature you will change, assembly boundaries. Before a second unnamed
lookup of your own, stop and send your open questions as scouts; "it's one quick grep" is what this
rule overrules. When one answer leads to the next, ask for the whole chain ("trace where apply
records a rect write and where that record is matched against the saved asset"), and keep working
on what you know while scouts run. After a red run, a scout groups its failures from the XML before
you read any.
Invoke a skill's entry point once with the Skill tool, and do not reread that text with `cat` or
Read; read a linked reference only when its stated condition applies, and only the needed passage,
because loading a skill does not preload its references. Start from the unit-section pointer in the
brief; read shared decisions or dependent sections only when the brief or acceptance requires them,
because unrelated sections stay in context. Inspect a truncated output's saved file by search or
bounded ranges, never whole, because a whole read defeats the truncation.
